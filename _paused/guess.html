How many steps do people think ahead?

If everyone guessed at random, the average would be about 50, so a reasonable guess is 33. But if everyone reasons that way, the target drops to 22. One more step gives 15, then 10, and so on down to 0. Zero is the equilibrium: the answer if everyone reasons all the way and expects everyone else to do the same.

People rarely go that far. In Rosemarie Nagel's experiments, first-round guesses averaged around 36. When the Danish newspaper Politiken ran the game in 2005 with 19,196 readers, the average was about 33, which put the winning number near 22. Behavioral economists call this level-k reasoning. The winning guess depends on how many steps other people take, so the game rewards predicting others as much as logic.

Nagel, R. (1995). Unraveling in guessing games: An experimental study. American Economic Review, 85(5), 1313–1326. Politiken figures as reported on Wikipedia.
