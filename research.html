Research

Publications

Information Provision and Vaccination Behavior

With Silvia Saccardo and Gretchen B. Chapman

In The SAGE Handbook of Health Psychology (2nd ed., Vol. 2, pp. 433–460). SAGE Publications, 2025.

Evidence synthesis from information provision interventions as a lever for vaccination. Applying a framework based on informational (e.g., risk communication) and non-informational elements (e.g., message source), we evaluate effects on beliefs, attitudes, intentions, and uptake, identify the conditions under which information is more or less likely to change behavior, and outline methodological priorities for future research.

Believability of Social Norm Messages and Vaccination Intentions

With Gretchen B. Chapman and Silvia Saccardo

Selective (In)attention: Evidence from Healthcare

With Andrea Low, Silvia Saccardo, Hengchen Dai, Craig Fox, Artin Galoosian, Richard Leuchter, Folasade May, Sitaram Vangala, and Jeff Fujimoto

Field and online experiments on how health communications designed to capture patients attention affect their willingness to engage with health information, including cases where interventions discourage rather than encourage engagement.

In progress

Misleading Expertise: Inferring Expertise from Source and Content Cues in Health Communication

With Gretchen B. Chapman and Silvia Saccardo

Experiments examining whether peripheral cues weakly diagnostic of competence, such as professional attire and technical jargon, substitute for credentials in inferences of expertise, and how such cues interact when presented jointly.

In progress

Leveraging the Intention-Action Framework to Close Cardiology Care Gaps

Amber Tang, Hengchen Dai, Jana Gallus, Richard Leuchter, Silvia Saccardo, Jose Arellano Martorellet, Sitaram Vangala, and UCLA Health collaborators

A field experiment testing the separate and joint effects of two behavioral interventions on physicians' actions to close their patients' cardiology care gaps, such as aspirin therapy.

With Dhwani Yagnaraman, as part of a megastudy led by Jan Pfänder and colleagues

Selected from 107 submissions for a US megastudy on trust in climate scientists, our intervention uses a value-aligned narrative and a delayed reveal to prompt reappraisal of climate scientists.
